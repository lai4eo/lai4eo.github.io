Harmonising global crop leaf‑area measurements

LAI4EO is an open consortium harmonising in-situ Leaf Area Index measurements over cropland into a free reference database for validating Sentinel-2-era satellite products. Contributors keep ownership of their data. The reference collections behind satellite LAI validation were built for kilometre pixels; this one is built for ten-metre ones.

Why this is needed

Built for kilometre pixels

The reference collections behind satellite LAI validation were assembled for kilometre-scale sensors, and they still reflect that design.

DIRECT V2.1, which underlies most product intercomparison, holds 280 LAI values from 176 sites, averaged over 3 km × 3 km so that the footprint comfortably exceeds the pixel being validated. Cropland supplies four-fifths of those values, but 161 come from China, and only two were collected after 2017, the year Sentinel-2 reached its two-satellite configuration.

Copernicus GBOV spans over 150 sites, but only 53 deliver LAI, and it draws on permanently instrumented sites rather than field campaigns.

Sentinel-2 and HLS observe cropland at 10 to 30 m, where a field-scale measurement corresponds to roughly one pixel rather than one part of a large average. At that scale, no comparable reference collection exists for cropland.

The measurements already exist

Research groups across the world hold multi-season LAI field campaigns that at present cannot be combined: different protocols, instruments, sampling geometries and phenological conventions, and no shared metadata standard.

LAI4EO harmonises them. The atomic record is the elementary sampling unit (ESU), carrying standardised geolocation and acquisition time, crop and phenology, LAI value and its dispersion, and the instrument, measurement type and protocol used to obtain it.

ESU footprints in the current release run from 6.25 to 225.0 m², a scale that matches decametric pixels.

Explore the database

The harmonised database holds 2567 ESU-level measurements across 1493 sampling units and 1145 field plots, gathered at 11 sites in 4 countries between 2014 and 2026 by 4 contributing groups.

What it holds

Crops. alfalfa, barley, emmer, maize, pea, rice, sorghum, soybean, sugar beet, wheat.

Instruments. AccuPAR LP-80, Hemispherical photography, LI-COR LAI-2000, LI-COR LAI-2200C, PocketLAI.

Metadata depth. BBCH phenology on 910 records, per-ESU standard deviation on 526, and RTK positions accurate to 0.03 m on 204.

Every harmonised record keeps a link back to the contributor’s original entry, and carries the contributor’s own quality flags.

How to get it

Starter Dataset v1.0 is the public slice: a subset of the harmonised database, deposited on Zenodo under CC BY 4.0. Free to anyone, no registration, citable by DOI.

The full database is open to Consortium members, who get it through the Harvest Portal ahead of the Data Paper. Contributing measurements is what makes you a member — see Join the Consortium below.

Contributors’ own deposits live in the LAI4EO Zenodo community, where each dataset stays under its authors’ names.

Join the Consortium

You keep your data. We do the harmonising.

Membership is free. There is no funding, no administrative structure and nothing legally binding — the terms are set out in the LAI4EO Collaboration Agreement, and you may withdraw at any time by email.

New to LAI4EO? The Letter of Invitation is the one-page version of what follows — the document to forward to a colleague, a data manager, or a head of department.

What you get

  • You keep ownership. No transfer of ownership, intellectual property, or control.
  • Named as a Consortium member on this site, in the Data Paper, and in every subsequent release.
  • Early access to the full harmonised database, before the Data Paper is published.
  • Harmonisation done for you, with your own quality flags preserved and a link back to your original record.
  • Co-authorship case by case for substantial datasets, and for anyone who helps compile, curate, analyse or write.

What we ask

  • Be reachable by email so we can interpret your data correctly. This is the main commitment.
  • Deposit on Zenodo under CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-NC 4.0.
  • Check your institutional, funder and national requirements before sharing.
  • Observe the embargo below, and keep Harvest Portal access inside the Consortium.

How to join

  1. Tell us what you hold. The form below asks for a short description — crops, instruments, roughly how many campaigns. A single season counts.
  2. Deposit your dataset on Zenodo under CC BY 4.0 or CC BY-NC 4.0, and submit it to the LAI4EO community. It stays yours, under your names.
  3. We harmonise it to the ESU schema and send it back to you. Nothing scientifically meaningful changes without your agreement.
  4. You are a member. Your group is listed on the Team page and you get access to the full database.

On the embargo. Until the Consortium’s first Data Paper is published, contributors agree not to publish analyses that combine data from two or more contributors. Analyses of your own data alone are unrestricted, at any time.

Who we are

LAI4EO was established by NASA Harvest and CNR-IREA. It runs without funding or administrative structure, and is open to any group willing to share data.

The institutions below host the Core Team, who maintain the harmonised database. They are not the list of contributing groups — any group that shares a dataset becomes a Consortium member, and that list grows with every release.

  • ICube Laboratory University of Strasbourg, France
  • CNR-IREA National Research Council, Milan, Italy
